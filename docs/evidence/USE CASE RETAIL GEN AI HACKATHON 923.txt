CONTEXT
Product returns are one of the most complex and costly parts of modern retail, particularly in footwear.
A return may look simple from the customer's perspective, but behind the scenes it can involve:
CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE 
AND INTERACTIONS
E-COMMERCE STORES
INVENTORY DISTRIBUTION CENTERS TRANSPORTATION
MERCHANDISING PRODUCT QUALITY FINANCE
FRAUD PREVENTION SUSTAINABILITY

--- Page 3 ---
The  reasons behind returns are equally varied. A product may not fit, may look different than expected, may arrive late or damaged, 
may simply no longer be wanted, or may be returned as a result of customer behaviors that negatively impact economics, including
abuse or fraud.
Most retailers still manage returns through a combination of rules, workflows and disconnected systems. Decisions are often made
without considering the full context of the customer, product, inventory position, logistics cost and likelihood of resale.
The business objective itself is simple: protect gross margin while improving customer satisfaction.
Agentic AI creates an opportunity to rethink this model as a whole.
An AI agent can potentially understand context, investigate information across multiple sources, make decisions, coordinate with
people or systems, take actions and learn from outcomes.

--- Page 4 ---
THE CHALLENGE
How could agentic AI fundamentally improve the return lifecycle for both customers and retailers?
Design and prototype an agentic solution that addresses a meaningful problem related to retail returns across stores and e-commerce.
The objective is not simply to automate an existing returns workflow.
We encourage teams to rethink how returns could work when AI agents are able to understand context, reason about complex 
situations, make decisions and take action.
SCOPE
The scope of this challenge is intentionally broad. Returns should be considered as an end-to-end business problem rather than a single 
transaction or process. Teams are expected to determine where agentic AI can create the most meaningful value and define the 
problem they choose to solve.
Identifying the opportunity is part of the challenge.

--- Page 5 ---
WHAT MAKES THIS AN AGENTIC AI CHALLENGE?
Strong solutions should demonstrate some combination of:
Understanding the end-to-end process, situation and objective
 Gathering information from multiple sources
Reasoning about possible actions and trade-offs
 Making or recommending decisions
Taking actions through APIs, tools or simulated business systems
 Coordinating multiple steps toward an objective
Knowing when human approval is required
 Evaluating whether an action worked
Adapting based on the result
 Demonstrating and defending a clear thesis for gross-margin protection and 
customer satisfaction
Teams may use one agent or multiple cooperating agents.
The use of generative AI alone does not make a solution agentic. We are looking for systems capable of reasoning and acting toward an objective, rather 
than simply generating content or answering questions.

YOUR SOLUTION
In the Ideation Phase 1: describe what your initial idea is for the development of a prototype to reduce returns ( see Phase 1 Instrucions)
Finalists in the Final Hacking Phase: Teams should develop a working prototype demonstrating how their solution would operate.
Your prototype does not need to integrate with real ALDO systems.
Simulated APIs, databases and workflows may be used.
We care more about the quality of the problem identified, the strength of the team's thesis, the agent's reasoning and the potential business impact than production-ready software.
Each team should demonstrate the following 5 :
1. PROBLEM AND THESIS
Clearly explain:
• What problem or opportunity did you identify?
• Who or what is affected?
• Why does it matter?
• Why is the current approach insufficient?
• What is your thesis for improving gross margin and/or customer satisfaction?
2. AGENTIC APPROACH
Show:
• What the agent knows
• What it is trying to accomplish
• What decisions it makes
• What tools, data or systems it can access
• What actions it can take
• Where human intervention remains appropriate

--- Page 7 ---
3. WORKING PROTOTYPE
Demonstrate at least one end-to-end scenario.
The prototype should make the agent's reasoning, decisions and actions visible 
enough to understand how the solution works.
4. BUSINESS IMPACT
Quantify or estimate the potential impact of the solution.
Teams should select the measures most relevant to their own thesis rather than 
optimize against a predefined set of KPIs.
5. RESPONSIBLE AI
Explain the risks associated with giving the proposed agent autonomy and how 
those risks would be managed.
Consider issues such as:
• Customer fairness
• Privacy
• Bias
• Explainability
• Financial authority
• Appropriate human oversight

TECHNICAL ENVIRONMENT
Teams may be provided with simulated APIs representing retail capabilities such as retrieving information and 
executing transactions.
Examples could include capabilities to:
• Retrieve customer, order, product and inventory information
• Calculate transportation options
• Initiate or modify a return
• Create an exchange
• Issue a refund
• Route merchandise
• Modify selected product information
The environment is intended to allow agents to take actions, not simply generate recommendations.
Teams are not required to use every dataset or capability provided.
